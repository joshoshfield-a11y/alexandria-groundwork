## Hardened spec

1. **State space:** all continuous paths x(t) from (x_a, t_a) to
   (x_b, t_b); the amplitude is a functional integral over this
   path space.
2. **Computable form:** the propagator
   K(x_b,t_b; x_a,t_a) = ∫ Dx(t) exp(iS[x]/ħ), S the classical
   action. Worked example: free particle,
   K = √(m/2πiħT) · exp(im(x_b−x_a)²/2ħT), T = t_b−t_a —
   the amplitude is largest near the classical straight-line
   path, with all paths contributing. Stationary-phase explains
   why: phases from non-classical paths oscillate rapidly and
   cancel, leaving the least-action path dominant — classical
   mechanics emerges from the sum.
3. **Measurable quantity:** transition probabilities |K|²,
   dimensionless; extracted from cross sections (barns) and
   decay rates (s⁻¹). Instrument: scattering detectors,
   interferometers (double-slit = two dominant paths).
4. **Standard theory:** Feynman's path-integral formulation of
   quantum mechanics (1948), equivalent to Schrödinger and
   Heisenberg pictures; Wick-rotated form underlies lattice
   field theory.
5. **Delta assessment:** none. This operator is the path integral —
   the amplitude as a sum over all histories weighted by the
   action phase, the formulation that makes quantum field theory
   computable — under a mythic name.
