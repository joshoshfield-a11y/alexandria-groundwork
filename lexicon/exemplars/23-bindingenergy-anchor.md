## Hardened spec

1. **State space:** a bound composite system and its separated constituents — a nucleus and its nucleons, an atom and its electrons, a molecule and its atoms.
2. **Computable form:** E_b = (Σ m_constituents − m_bound)·c², the mass defect times c²: the energy that must be supplied to disassemble the system. Worked example: the deuteron. m_p = 938.272 MeV/c², m_n = 939.565 MeV/c², m_d = 1875.613 MeV/c². The defect is 938.272 + 939.565 − 1875.613 = 2.224 MeV/c², so E_b ≈ 2.22 MeV — the energy of the gamma ray emitted when a proton and neutron fuse, and the energy a photon needs to photodisintegrate the deuteron.
3. **Measurable quantity:** energy, in electronvolts (eV, keV, MeV, GeV) or joules. Instrument: Penning-trap mass spectrometry for the mass defect; gamma spectroscopy for the energy released on formation; photodisintegration threshold measurements.
4. **Standard theory:** special relativity (mass–energy equivalence) applied in nuclear and atomic physics; the semi-empirical mass formula for the systematics across nuclei.
5. **Delta assessment:** none. This operator is the standard binding energy under a mythic name.
