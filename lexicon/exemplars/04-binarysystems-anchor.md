## Hardened spec

1. **State space:** phase space of two gravitating point masses m₁, m₂
   with relative position r and relative momentum p (reduced mass
   μ = m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂), separation vector r = r₂ − r₁).
2. **Computable form:** Kepler's third law, T² = 4π²a³/G(m₁+m₂),
   where a is the semi-major axis and T the orbital period.
   Worked example: Earth–Moon, a = 3.844×10⁸ m,
   m₁+m₂ ≈ 6.047×10²⁴ kg, G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ m³kg⁻¹s⁻² →
   T ≈ 2.357×10⁶ s ≈ 27.28 d, the unperturbed two-body value
   (the observed sidereal month is 27.3217 d; solar perturbations
   account for the small difference).
3. **Measurable quantity:** orbital period (s), semi-major axis (m),
   mass ratio (dimensionless). Instruments: astrometric tracking,
   radial-velocity spectroscopy, pulsar timing (for relativistic
   binaries, e.g. Hulse–Taylor at ~7.75 h).
4. **Standard theory:** the Newtonian two-body problem; Kepler's
   laws (empirically), with general-relativistic corrections for
   compact binaries (perihelion precession, orbital decay via
   gravitational radiation).
5. **Prediction differing from standard theory:** none.
